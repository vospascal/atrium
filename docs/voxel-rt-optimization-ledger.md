# voxel-rt — Optimization Ledger

**The scoreboard.** One row per optimization idea that has ever been on the table
for this engine — from `voxel-sandbox`'s mesh era, from the Shadertoy technique
bank, from the xima dossier, from devlog sources — with a status and, where it
was measured, the number that decided it.

Status vocabulary:

- ✅ **USED** — shipped and on by default in voxel-rt today.
- 🎚️ **LEVER** — implemented, measured, shipped **off**; kept runnable because an
  M3 Max loss can be a Quest win (Pascal's variant/lever hygiene rule).
- 🔜 **OPEN** — not built; real candidate, tagged with its experiment slot.
- ❌ **DEAD** — cannot apply here, or measured and closed. Reason given.

Numbers are M3 Max @ 2560×1440 from the headless harness
(`docs/voxel-rt-bench.md`). Companion docs: `voxel-rt-plan.md` (the ladder),
`voxel-rt-technique-bank.md` (Shadertoy menu, T-numbers),
`voxel-optimization-candidates.md` (the sandbox/mesh-era list this ledger
triages), `voxel-rt-research-dossier.md` (published papers/engines, R-numbers —
the source and reasoning behind every row tagged **R1–R11**).

### The one principle that predicts these verdicts

**Direct light is high-frequency and needs accurate visibility; indirect light is
smooth gradation, which masks visibility error.** Published result — Yu, Cox, Kim,
Ritschel, Grosch, Dachsbacher & Kautz, *Perceptual influence of approximate
visibility in indirect illumination*, ACM TAP 6(4):24, 2009; it is the stated
motivation behind Imperfect Shadow Maps and is cited by VGI (2.18) for exactly
this purpose.

Worth writing down because it retroactively explains two verdicts we reached
*empirically and separately*: the per-brick distance field was unusable for
penumbrae (2.11) while the same order of geometric coarseness is invisible in the
0.5 m GI volume (2.13). That looked like two unrelated results. It is one
principle — the lattice landed in the **direct** term, where nothing masks it.

Operationally: coarseness is cheap to spend on indirect terms and expensive to
spend on direct ones. Check which side a proposed approximation lands on *before*
building it, rather than discovering it per lever.

| # | Technique | Status | Evidence / note |

| 1.1 | Two-level sparse brickmap (8³ bricks, empty bricks skipped) | ✅ USED | S1. **100,865 occupied bricks, seed-1 island (re-measured 2026-08-02; it read 71,941 before the world moved to one material per 1 m block).** Note what that number now means: every one of them is fully solid and single-material, so level 1 is empty and the second level exists only for edits |
| 1.2 | **Chebyshev distance-field skip** (bindings 9/10) | ✅ USED | S2 headline win, **17–27% under baseline**. | 1.8 | Brick bit-grid | 🎚️ LEVER | Redundant next to the distance byte; its data is read by 2.6. Retry where caches are small |
| 1.12 | **Uniform-brick tag** (one material in all 512 cells → hit at the brick face) | ✅ USED | Level-0 pointers carry a 2-bit tag; a uniform brick needs no descent and no level-1 storage. **57.9% of occupied bricks qualify: 6.4% faster in scenario A, 10.1% in C, and 45.2 → 21.9 MB.** No lever exists *by design* — tag and fast path are one data format, so "off" means building different data (`bench_dda --no-collapse`). | 1.13 | **AADF directional bounds** (six per-axis bounds, jump the box they span) | 🎚️ LEVER | Slower in all four scenarios (A 5.011 vs 4.748, C 4.565 vs 4.408). | 1.9 | **Brick-grid mip pyramid** (distant rays step coarser) | 🔜 OPEN | The one *real* ray-tracer form of sandbox candidate C. Distinct from 2.7, which lost. No slot yet. **Design constraint, borrowed from alpha-tested mipmapping (R7): max-downsample the DISTANCE FIELD, never average the coverage** — averaging is exactly what makes thin geometry evaporate at coarse levels (the same mechanism as E4's canopy leak, wanted there, fatal here). Composes with 1.13: a *directional* field mipped by max is the conservative form of this row, and 1.13's data already exists behind a lever |
| 1.10 | Frustum culling | ❌ DEAD | Per-pixel rays are perfect culling by construction — nothing to cull |
| 1.11 | **Workgroup-cooperative brick traversal** (shared-memory prefetch) | 🔜 OPEN | Untested, no number. Adjacent pixels' rays walk near-identical brick sequences, so a workgroup could fetch each brick once into shared memory instead of once per lane. This is the ray-tracer form of sandbox's "group work by coherence" (5.10's motivation, not its implementation). Bench before believing |
| 1.15 | **SVO / SVDAG / SSVDAG / TSVDAG** (hierarchical subtree fusion under transformations) | ❌ DEAD, structurally | **R11.** Read in full (Molenaar & Eisemann, I3D 2025). Priced out twice. **(a) Static by construction** — the paper's own §1 says so; translation search is *"a couple of hours"* per 64K³ scene after their O(NV²)→O(NV) fix, with no incremental update anywhere, against E2's **4.9 ms** republish and 3.6's word-patch materialization. **(b) Its own render benchmark argues against compressing at all**: *"an SVDAG with fixed 32-bit pointer encoding outperforms variable pointer length methods… by about 10%"*, their LUT+Huffman costs **+12%** frame time and translations **+30%** (Bistro ≈28 → ≈41 ms), blamed on variable-length reads and traversal-stack register pressure. **That is 1.2/1.13's load-count lesson arriving from an independent direction** — our two fixed levels with one 32-bit tagged pointer already sit at the fast end of the axis they measured. They also confirm our own negative: a hardware BVH over the top levels *"reduces performance"*. Editable-DAG line, if ever asked again: Careil et al. 2020, Molenaar & Eisemann 2024 |
| 1.14 | NAADF's per-VOXEL in-cell AADF form (distances bounded by the parent cell) | ❌ DEAD by arithmetic | **R1.** The form the paper uses at its innermost layer, priced against our fanout rather than theirs: they nest 4³ voxels in 4³ blocks, we use 8³ bricks, so a per-voxel field is 512 entries × ~18–24 bits ≈ **1.2–1.5 KB per brick against our 64 B occupancy mask — roughly 100 MB**. 1.13 tested the *brick*-level form instead, which is the only rung where the memory works, and that already lost on load count. **The transferable half of the paper landed as 1.12, not as either directional row** |

## 5. Sandbox mesh-era techniques — mostly dead by construction

Most of these optimize **triangle submission**: draw calls, vertex bandwidth,
the GPU vertex stage. voxel-rt has no meshes and no triangles (an explicit plan
non-goal), and its cost is ray steps + memory latency. They are not "rejected" —
there is no analogue to port. They remain live and correct in `voxel-sandbox`.

**Audit correction, 2026-07-30** (prompted by Pascal asking *why* each one is
dead — the right question to ask of a blanket verdict): two rows here were
misfiled. **Chunked meshes** and **vertex packing** are not dead, they are
*shipped under different names* (1.1 and 3.1) — the mesh implementation is
inapplicable but the technique landed. Re-deriving the rest per-item also
surfaced two open candidates that a blanket "no triangles" answer had hidden:
**1.11** (workgroup-cooperative traversal) and **3.5** (per-brick local material
palette). Blanket verdicts hide analogues; each row now carries its own reason.

| # | Technique | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | 2D greedy merging (both axes) | ❌ DEAD — DDA derives the hit face from the crossed axis in O(1); a 1-voxel wall and a 1000-voxel wall cost the same. The *idea* (exploit contiguity) survives twice: merging **empty** space = the distance field (1.2), merging **solid** space = interior culling (3.3) |
| 5.2 | Hidden / interior **face** culling | ❌ DEAD — the ray terminates on first hit, so interior faces are never *visited*. Free by construction, no mechanism needed. Memory analogue is 3.3 |
| 5.3 | Mesh groups (Terrain / Cover / Canopy) | ❌ DEAD — exists to stop non-mergeable geometry poisoning a chunk's merge and to vary material/shadow flags. Nothing to poison; a material byte → palette lookup differentiates per voxel at zero structural cost |
| 5.4 | Chunked meshes, not per-voxel | ✅ **USED as 1.1** *(corrected 2026-07-30 — was misfiled here)*. The lesson is "amortize per-unit overhead over a spatial block"; the brickmap is exactly that. Only the mesh implementation is inapplicable |
| 5.5 | Vertex compression / packing (40 B → ~8 B per vertex) | ✅ **USED as 3.1** *(corrected 2026-07-30 — was misfiled here)*. "Pack per-element data as tight as it goes" is applied to the voxel buffers. Still has headroom → 3.5 |
| 5.6 | 16-bit index buffers | ❌ DEAD — no indices. *(Was already reverted in sandbox: mixed index formats split the batch set key)* |
| 5.7 | Tight AABBs for frustum culling | ❌ DEAD — see 1.10 |
| 5.8 | One shared material → bevy batching | ❌ DEAD — no bevy, no materials, one compute pipeline |
| 5.9 | Grass clump instancing | ❌ DEAD in this form — the voxel-rt analogue is domain-tiled SDF instancing (6.6) |
| 5.10 | Per-face-direction mesh split (6 meshes/chunk) | ❌ DEAD — no vertex shader to skip. Its *motivation* (group work by coherence) is alive as 1.11 |
| 5.11 | Octree / variable-size chunks | ❌ DEAD in this form — the analogue is 1.9 |
| 5.12 | Multi-draw indirect / SSBO chunk positions / `gl_DrawID` | ❌ DEAD — no draw calls at all |
| 5.13 | Hand-rolled buffer suballocation | ❌ DEAD — we own our buffers directly; no allocator to beat |
| 5.14 | Texture atlasing / bindless arrays / GPU texture compression | ❌ DEAD — no albedo textures anywhere; colour is a palette lookup |
| 5.15 | `NotShadowCaster` confetti + solid inner shadow shell | ❌ DEAD — no shadow-caster concept; shadows are rays |

**The one genuinely transferable lesson from this block** is not a technique but
a reframing: *anything that varies per unit fragments the fast path.* In sandbox
that was the batch set key; here it is pipeline permutations — which is why E1c
precompiles the 3 distinct preset pipelines (≈4.0 ms at startup, 67 µs to
re-prewarm) instead of branching at runtime.

Hand-drawn and generated detail then share cells and compose, instead of one being resampled onto the other and both looking slightly wrong. Costs nothing to decide now and is expensive to retrofit |
| 6.27 | **A patterned emitter injects its MEAN — near field vs far field, not an approximation** | ✅ USED — S2c. CAGI holds a 3-bit emitter index per half-metre cell and cannot carry per-texel structure, so emissive specks looked like they could never light anything. But the light arriving *elsewhere* from a speckled surface **is** its average emission times its area: detail matters to the eye on the surface, only the mean escapes it. So the pixel gets the full pattern and the volume gets `mean_emitted_radiance`, obtained by evaluating the CPU pattern reference over the six faces weighted by face count — numerically, so a future generator cannot inherit a wrong closed form. The general lesson: **when a coarse consumer cannot represent a fine signal, ask what that consumer actually needs from it** rather than treating the coarseness as a limitation to apologise for |
| 6.28 | **Reduce at the seam: pass what the consumer needs, not what you have** | ✅ USED — S2c, and it is what made the fix cheap. The CAGI attribute builders read the COMPILED material table, so a live edit could never reach the GI bounce. Passing `&[Material]` down fails on the incremental path: it runs on the world thread and would need an **8 KB copy per 2.8 us edit**. But the builders never wanted materials — they wanted *"material id -> packed attribute word"*. `MaterialAttributes` is exactly that: **104 bytes and `Copy`**, so it rides inside a `VoxelEdit` across the thread boundary for free. | 6.23 | **Distance fade derived from the period, not authored** | ✅ USED — S2. So the fade is expressed in **periods** (250 of them) and the metric distance follows from the layer — one global lever instead of a hand-derived distance per layer, all of them wrong. Same shape as the sandbox's jitter-minification fade. Deliberately keyed off the period and NOT off 6.25's texel size: fading on texel size would erase a 1 m band because its texels are small |
| 6.24 | **A shader const for a real-valued knob, deliberately paying a recompile** | ✅ USED — S2's pattern strength. It is dragged a handful of times per session and naga can fold a zero-strength layer away entirely, so a const beats a uniform read in the hottest shading path. The knobs that ARE dragged while looking — the per-material `amount` sliders — live in the material table, which uploads with no rebuild. The split is by *how often a knob moves*, not by what type it is |
| 6.29 | **A by-value struct copy that is then dynamically indexed spills to scratch — read through the binding** | ✅ USED — S3, and it is the single biggest shading win the arc has produced: **pattern cost 6.713 → 2.972 ms (−56%), whole DDA pass −43%, entry cost 5.55 → 1.32 ms**, with **all 28 bench PNGs byte-identical**. | 7.11 | **Convergence quoted in FRAMES is a frame-rate-dependent simulation** | 🔜 OPEN observation (found 2026-07-31, not fixed) | Surfaced by **R9** while pricing its (dead) method. E4's CA runs N iterations *per frame*, so its 32-frames-to-bit-exact is **1.07 s at 30 fps, 0.53 s at 60, 0.44 s at 72 Hz, 0.27 s at 120** — light propagation speed is a function of frame rate, and a preset or platform switch changes it. **The plan found the same class of bug independently in E10** (*"a 10-frame window at 72 Hz is 140 ms"*), which is what makes it a row instead of a footnote. Not urgent for GI — E5 records Pascal running the 32-frame lag and not being bothered. | 7.16 | **A coarse proxy needs a rule PER FIELD — one election cannot serve them all** | ✅ USED — the embedded-emitter fix. A glow block in a one-voxel wall had a 1-in-16 chance of working. `fold_voxel_attribute` now runs two rules in one word: last-wins for albedo/transmittance, **sticky** for the emitter. **E5 never saw it because its gate placed glow blocks in open air**, where the block is its cell's only occupant and always wins — a reminder that a gate exercising the easy placement proves nothing about the embedded one. | 7.15 | **A feature can be correct and invisible — check the CONDITIONS before the code** | ✅ USED — S2d. Patterned emitters worked, injected the right mean, and could not be seen. Two one-line-ish exposures, no new machinery. The operational form: when a mechanism verifies but does not read, ask what the *observation conditions* are before re-reading the mechanism — and specifically, check that every quantity a feature needs is **authorable in the range it needs**, not just wired. Corollary from the second fix's first attempt: when a widget's RANGE is wrong, widen the range — do not throw away the widget. Picking a hue and exceeding 1.0 are separate problems, so a picker plus an intensity beats three raw channels, and folding the product at the upload boundary means the extra field costs no bytes and no shader change |
| 7.14 | **Documenting a mechanism is not testing it — a described tier can be inert** | ✅ USED (the hard way) — S0 wrote a two-tier liveness model into the material panel's own hover text: edits live in direct shading, stale in the GI bounce "until a re-pack". The tiers were real; **the re-pack read the COMPILED table**, so it recomputed the values it already had and a material edit could never reach the bounce at all. The prose was confident, detailed, and wrong for the entire life of S0-S2, because nothing asserted that a *changed* row produced a *changed* attribute word. Fixed in S2c with `a_live_edited_albedo_reaches_the_cell_attributes`. Sibling of 7.3's lesson at the data layer: **an invariant you only wrote down in a doc comment is not an invariant** |
| 7.12 | **A dead row's REASON expires; re-triage on infrastructure changes, not on new ideas** | ✅ USED — this batch | R3 and R4 were dead on "we have no history, no G-buffer, no reprojection". E10's non-goal amendment (2026-07-31) invalidated that sentence in a day without either source changing. Both are still dead — now on *dependencies and Quest reachability* — but the verdicts had to be re-derived, not re-read. Operationally: when a stage lands new **infrastructure** (E10a's G-buffer, E7b's HDR), grep the ❌ rows for reasons that named its absence |
| 7.13 | **A paper's transferable half is often not its headline** | ✅ USED — R1/NAADF, the clearest case yet | The paper is *named after* its directional distance fields. We built them (1.13) and they **lost in all four scenarios** — while a structural detail the paper does not advertise, collapsing single-material cells so they are hit at the entry face, won **6.4–10.1% AND took the brickmap from 45.2 MB to 21.9 MB** (1.12). Two operational consequences: read a paper's *data layout* as carefully as its algorithm, and when the headline loses, do not close the source — 1.14 (their per-voxel form) is dead by our fanout, but the collapse idea was sitting next to it. Corollary about our own numbers: a loss can be a loss *for one consumer only* — 1.13's field genuinely beats chebyshev on reach and is now the first revival door for 2.11 |
| 7.17 | **Reasoning by analogy from a real win is a hypothesis, not a plan** | ✅ USED (as a rejection) — S3 | The row-copy fix (6.29) paid **1.9 ms** for removing redundant work from a loop. Hoisting the four redundant per-layer salt hashes looks like exactly the same shape, so it got built and measured: **0.002 ms**. The two are not the same. The row copy was dear because of **where the data lived** — spilled to scratch — not because it repeated; four extra ALU hashes repeat but never leave registers. Banked as a DOCUMENTED NEGATIVE with two siblings from the same session: stubbing out the animation plumbing entirely (**7.431 vs 7.430 — 0.00**) and deleting unused generators from the binary (**6.106 vs 6.140 — 0.00**, dead switch arms are already free). The operational half: when a fix works, write down *why* it worked at the mechanism level, or the next thing that resembles it will get built on faith |
| 7.18 | **A band, a threshold or a verdict needs three runs; a number needs one** | ✅ USED (the hard way) — S3 | Section 11's generator price table shipped from a single run. Re-measured, the spread is only ±0.07 ms on the dear rows and ±0.02 on the cheap ones — which changes no band anywhere **except at a threshold**, where it changes the answer: `tile-edge` read 0.065 / 0.052 / 0.046 across three runs, straddling the 0.05 Free/Cheap boundary, so the studio's cost pip was showing a band that noise picked. Now median-of-three, with the instability written on the constant itself. The general rule: **run-to-run noise is harmless for a continuous number and decisive for a discretised one**, so anything that quantises a measurement — a band, a pass/fail gate, a ships-on/ships-off verdict — needs the sample count that its threshold demands |
| 7.19 | **An assertion written against a measured rate goes VACUOUS when the rate hits an endpoint** | ✅ USED — S3, found while chasing a phantom bug | `uniform_bricks_collapse_and_the_survivors_are_all_sculpted` asserted `uniform > unique / 2`, chosen when the collapse rate measured 58.6%. The world later moved to one material per authored block, every brick became uniform, `unique` went to **0** — and `uniform > 0 / 2` is trivially true, so the test passed silently through a change that made a 5× difference to frame time and invalidated every recorded section-1 number. **A ratio assertion must be checked against its endpoints**: `> half` says nothing when the denominator can reach zero. Assert both sides. The same change is why the ledger's own 1.12 collapse figures (57.9%, 71,941 bricks) are now historical |
