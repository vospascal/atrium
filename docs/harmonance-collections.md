## How the site frames every collection

The same stance on every page, worth keeping for our own notes: each tone is a *listening
prompt* or experiment, its name describes the tradition behind it, not a promised result.
Planetary pitches are octave translations of orbital or rotational periods; angel-number
meanings are modern numerology; solfeggio associations are a modern listening tradition.
Suggested practice: pick one tone, keep the volume comfortable, start with a short session,
notice your response without expecting one, compare two tones at the same level before
choosing. Every page carries the same safety line: low volume, stop on discomfort, care with
tinnitus or sensitive hearing, never while driving, not a medical device.

## Harmonics: the rule, as the site states it

- One tone with its own lower octaves beneath it (an octave down is exactly half the
  frequency), heard as one full voice rather than a chord. Example: 528 Hz over 264, 132 and
  66 Hz.
- **Up to three octaves below the tone, none lower than 60 Hz**, so low tones carry fewer
  lines. Each line is set to sound as loud as the others (the levels measured from two pages
  are in focus-tones.md: the lower the line, the higher its level).
- **The drift**: about every 24 s the weight passes from the tone down through each octave
  and back, each line rising and falling by about 8 dB in turn. Nothing pulses or beats.
- **Tones together**: 12 listens play tones side by side with no octaves added, each tone
  leading in turn, highest first: 174 + 417 + 741; 285 + 528 + 852; 396 + 639 + 963;
  432 + 528; 144.72 + 396; 888 + 183.58; 183.58 + 963; 126.22 + 528; 126.22 + 417;
  963 + 172.06; 141.27 + 741; 432 + 174 Hz.
- Plays for as long as you listen. The site's own summary of the evidence: octaves are plain
  acoustics; claims that particular tones change body or mind have little support.

## Two guides, read 2026-11-23

Summaries in our own words, with the measured numbers the guides cite (sources named so
they can be checked).

### Sound baths (`/resources/sound-baths-what-to-expect-and-how-to-listen-safely`)

- **What one is**: about an hour lying down while someone plays long ringing tones (singing
  bowls, gongs, chimes, small bells), letting each fade before the next. In the largest
  study (Goldsby et al., 62 adults) instruments came in a set, repeating order (tingshas,
  Tibetan bowls, bells, crystal bowls, gongs, bowls again), placed near the listeners'
  heads. In a Chilean trial (Rio-Alamos et al.) a music therapist went from high bowls to
  low.
- **A bowl's partials are not harmonic**, measured in the Chilean trial:

  | Bowl | Main tone | Overtones |
  |---|---|---|
  | 23 cm | 99 Hz | 263, 465, 737, 971 Hz |
  | 15 cm | 219 Hz | 603, 1115, 1713, 2515 Hz |
  | 12 cm | 465 Hz | 1185, 2224, 3471, 4865 Hz |
  | 8 cm | 927 Hz | 1879, 2401, 4437 Hz |

  In the three larger bowls the first overtone is 2.55–2.75 times the main tone (a string's
  would be exactly 2).
- **Why a bowl swells**: no bowl is perfectly round, so each mode splits into two a few hertz
  apart that beat (Terwagne & Bush). Kim & Choi measured a 260 mm bowl: main mode
  482.61 Hz with a weaker twin 6.68 Hz away, one swell every 0.15 s, ringing for more than
  40 s and dying away by about 50 s. That swell is an acoustic (monaural) beat, heard from a
  speaker too, unlike a binaural one.
- **Gongs** are non-linear: struck softly they ring steadily, struck hard the vibration turns
  chaotic into a wash (Chaigne, Touzé & Thomas); some glide in pitch after the strike, a
  large Chinese opera gong down by up to three semitones, smaller curved gongs upward.
- **History**: no evidence singing bowls are ancient or Tibetan; they enter the written
  record in 1972 (Hennings & Wolff, *Tibetan Bells*), per Grimes in Tricycle.
- **Evidence**: small studies report people feeling calmer (less tension, lower heart rate,
  a little less sleepy), but no control group in the largest, high risk of bias in the
  reviews (Stanhope & Weinstein 2020: "cannot recommend"; two 2025 reviews could not pool).
  Oomen et al. played bowls at 40, 73 and 110 Hz (capped near 75 dB) and found no effect of
  pitch; arousal rose for 15 minutes, then fell until 40, whatever the pitch.
- **Loudness**: no study measured a typical sound bath's level. WHO safe listening for
  adults: 80 dB average for 40 h a week, each +5 dB cutting the time to under a third
  (one hour uses 2.5 % of the week at 80 dB, 25 % at 90, 80 % at 95, 300 % at 100);
  children: 75 dB for 40 h.

For Atrium: the bowl data (inharmonic partials, a split mode beating a few hertz apart, a
40–50 s ring) is the measured physics a singing-bowl sound would need, and the site's
Studio offers a singing bowl as an ambient layer. Not built; generator work.

### Alpha waves and binaural beats (`/resources/alpha-waves-and-binaural-beats-present-awareness`)

- **Alpha**: the brain's 8–13 Hz rhythm, strongest over the back of the head, awake with
  eyes closed and relaxed, blocked by visual attention and effort (IFCN glossary). Found by
  Hans Berger (first human EEG 1924, published 1929), confirmed by Adrian & Matthews 1934.
- **Eyes closed multiplies it**: harmonance's own analysis of an open PhysioNet dataset (109
  volunteers, one minute eyes open and one closed, electrodes O1/Oz/O2): 106 of 109 had more
  alpha with eyes closed, median 3.5 times; median eyes-closed peak 10.25 Hz, the middle half
  9.5–10.6 Hz, range 7.75–13 Hz. Haegens et al. (MEG, 51 people): mean 10.3 Hz, rising with
  task load.
- **Binaural beats**: described by Dove in 1839, explained by Oster (Scientific American
  1973); heard only when the tones are below about 1000 Hz; the percept likely forms in the
  brainstem (superior olivary nuclei).
- **Does the brain follow the beat?** Ingendoh et al.'s systematic review: 14 EEG studies,
  5 in line with entrainment, 8 not, 1 mixed; for alpha, 2 found more alpha and 4 no
  difference. A confound: most listened with eyes closed, which alone raises alpha 3.5-fold.
  On tasks, Garcia-Argibay et al.'s meta-analysis (22 studies) reported a medium average
  effect, g = 0.45, on memory, attention and other measures, across many beat rates.
- **Their Falling Alpha Session**, measured from its master: left 108 Hz throughout, right
  120 → 117 Hz in a straight line, largest deviation from the planned line 0.00038 Hz;
  loudness −23 LUFS, peaks more than 21 dB below full scale. The beat passes 10.25 Hz
  5 h 15 min in.
- **Their listening advice**: headphones; well below conversation level for nine hours; try
  ten minutes eyes closed in silence, then ten with the session, and compare.

For Atrium: our 12 → 9 Hz session matches theirs (measured in docs/binaural-beats.md). Their
master's −23 LUFS is a reference for how loud they make these pieces.
